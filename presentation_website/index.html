IDR-Navigator: Bounded Inertial Dead Reckoning on Commodity Smartphones Without Vehicle Telemetry

When consumer vehicles enter subterranean tunnels, dense urban canyons, or highway underpasses, Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) carrier locks degrade or vanish entirely. Classical strapdown inertial navigation on uncalibrated smartphone MEMS sensors diverges quadratically within seconds due to bias instability. IDR-Navigator replaces quadratic double integration with a five-stage hybrid estimation cascade—combining a 6-DoF attitude observer, spectral vibration gating, a causal Squeeze-and-Excitation Temporal Convolutional Network (SE-TCN), Zero-Velocity Updates (ZUPT), and offline OpenStreetMap vector constraints.

§ 02 — ARCHITECTURE

Five-Stage Estimation Cascade

Rather than integrating noisy body-frame acceleration (&iint; a(t) dt²), IDR-Navigator reformulates dead reckoning as a sequential velocity-estimation and topological projection problem running natively on the smartphone CPU/NPU.

Why Naive IMU Fails

A 0.02 m/s² uncompensated accelerometer bias accumulates 36 meters of error in 60 seconds and over 144 meters in two minutes. Bounding error requires learning velocity directly from multi-axis dynamics.

STAGE 01

6-DoF Attitude Observer & Frame Alignment

100 Hz · SO(3) Manifold

Smartphones are placed arbitrarily in cupholders, dashboard mounts, or handlebar clamps. An adaptive Mahony complementary filter fuses tri-axial gyroscope angular rates with low-pass gravitational acceleration vectors to maintain a continuous orientation quaternion. Dynamic PCA over horizontal acceleration during pre-outage GNSS lock resolves the yaw misalignment angle between the phone's silicon axes and the vehicle's forward heading vector.

Input: Raw a_b, ω_b ∈ ℝ³ (100 Hz) Output: Gravity-compensated vehicle-frame kinematics
STAGE 02

Spectral Vibration & Jerk Gate

Savitzky-Golay · 128-Sample Window

Internal combustion engines and two-wheeler chassis generate high-amplitude harmonic vibrations (18–65 Hz) that mimic forward acceleration. Our spectral gate computes short-time energy ratios between vehicle locomotion bands (0.3–8.0 Hz) and structural resonance bands, suppressing phantom speed surges and road bump shocks before neural inference.

Input: Windowed acceleration a_v[t-128 : t] Output: Jerk-gated motion confidence mask
STAGE 03

Causal SE-TCN Forward Velocity Estimator

INT8 ONNX · 2.1 MB · 20 Hz Inference

At the core of the pipeline, an 8-channel causal Temporal Convolutional Network with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) channel attention ingests a 4.0-second receptive field of gravity-removed IMU streams. Using exponentially dilated causal convolutions (d ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}), the network regresses instantaneous forward speed directly from road-surface micro-excitations—converting quadratic error growth into bounded linear error.

Input: Tensor [1 × 8 × 40] INT8/FP32 Output: Estimated velocity v̂(t) (m/s) & uncertainty σ²
STAGE 04

Zero-Velocity Updates (ZUPT) & Non-Holonomic Constraints

Error-State Kalman Filter

Wheeled vehicles obey non-holonomic physical constraints: lateral velocity (v_y ≈ 0) and vertical velocity (v_z ≈ 0) in the vehicle frame are tightly bounded. When the stopping detector confirms a standstill at urban traffic signals, ZUPT clamps velocity to zero instantly and calibrates gyroscope heading bias without relying on GPS Doppler.

Input: v̂(t), γ_vib, ω_z Output: Drift-clamped 2D velocity vector & corrected heading
STAGE 05

Offline OSM Road Vector Snapping

Topological Map-Matching · 1D Manifold

Unconstrained dead reckoning on prolonged bypasses can slowly accumulate azimuth drift. Stage 5 projects the dead-reckoned increments onto cached OpenStreetMap (OSM) highway polylines. This collapses the navigation search space from 2D/3D to a 1D roadway corridor, holding cross-track error to zero while neural speed governs along-track progression.

Input: Dead-reckoned position + cached OSM spatial index Output: Map-clamped geodetic coordinates

Arterial & Tunnel Stability (0.43% – 0.80%)

In steady-state highway corridors, road micro-vibration signatures correlate tightly with forward wheel speed. Combined with low yaw variance and 1D topological snapping, the causal SE-TCN achieves sub-1% drift over 1.6 kilometers.

Two-Wheeler Roll Dynamics (9.08%)

Motorcycles lean into turns up to 25°–30°, causing lateral acceleration to align with the frame's apparent gravity vector. Under severe handlebar vibration and traffic filtering over 840 m, drift naturally rises to 9.08% but stays reliably within ISRO's 10% ceiling.
